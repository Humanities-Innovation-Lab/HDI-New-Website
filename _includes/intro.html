Humanities Data Inquiry

Humanities Data Inquiry (HDI) studies how researchers in the “traditional” Humanities produce, use, and share the materials that information professionals call data—and why the digital tools, policies, and infrastructure built to manage research data have been so slowly adopted across the Humanities.

Most efforts to close this gap ask how Humanities researchers should change to fit contemporary Research Data Management (RDM) and Research Data Infrastructure (RDI). HDI turns the question around. We begin from the premise that the Humanities already have long-standing, robust—if often intuitive and unspoken—traditions for collecting, organizing, preserving, and circulating research materials. Our goal is to understand those traditions on their own terms, and to use that understanding to make data management and infrastructure work better for the Humanities, rather than the other way around.

Hosted by the University of Lethbridge Humanities Innovation Lab, HDI combines historical, ethnographic, and comparative research to build a clearer, evidence-based picture of Humanities data practice—past and present.
